The Lady of Saint-Pol

A noblewoman at the court of the mad King Charles VI, 1400 to 1422.

You are seventeen, of a middling house, and your father is negotiating your marriage. The king is mad and the princes are about to tear France in half. You cannot command anyone. Every half-year you choose where to live, at court in Paris or on your estates, and what to do there, and almost everything you do goes through someone else: a patron's household, your steward, your chamberwoman, a lawyer, a messenger. They can be slow, careless or false.

The game

  • Seven scenes in 1400 decide your marriage, then 44 half-years from 1401 to 1422, each a choice of court or estates and two to four of 22 actions.
  • History is fixed: the murder of Orléans, the butchers' rising, Agincourt, the Treaty of Troyes. Six times Paris changes masters, and your ties to each side decide what it costs you. Warnings come first, mixed with false ones.
  • Your own story runs alongside: 37 story scenes and 13 short ones with your husband, your friend Ysabel, your rival Jacqueline, Christine de Pizan, the queen, and your father's house of Brécourt. Choices made at seventeen come back at forty.
  • It ends in October 1422, or sooner if you die or your creditors take the house. Your legacy is the house's lands, your children and their matches, your good name, your standing at court, your wealth, and your own fate.

About it

  • About an hour and a half for a whole life. The game saves after every choice.
  • Four family starts (an old country house, a Picard house among Burgundians, a house in the Orléans service, a new house of the Parlement) and three difficulties. On the middle difficulty, about four careful players in five see the end of the reign.
  • Plays in the browser, on a phone or a computer. Installs from the web version and plays offline.
  • Saves in your browser. The menu's save code moves a game between devices.
  • Free. No account, no ads, no analytics. The page fetches nothing: system fonts, art and sound drawn in code.
