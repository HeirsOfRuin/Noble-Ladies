# christine, seed 3

## Lent, 1400: Reputation

> A lady's good name is the first thing she owns and the last thing she should spend. It is built slowly, by many small proofs, and lost in an afternoon.

Two offers for your hand have come to your father, Messire Enguerrand de Brécourt. One is from Messire Robert d'Avelin; the other from Messire Hue de Cramailles. Both houses will send men to the Easter feast in your father's hall, and those men will report on you. You are seventeen. Officially, none of this is your affair.

**Chose:** Keep your eyes down, speak little, be seen at prayer

## May, 1400: Favour

> At court every kindness is a debt and every debt is remembered. Choose whose company you keep, because others will choose for you by watching it.

Your mother takes you to Paris for a month, to the Hôtel Saint-Pol and the great houses around it. The king is well this spring, and the court is full. Three circles of ladies will receive a girl of good family. You can only be seen to belong to one.

**Chose:** The queen's ladies

## June, 1400: Information

> Know a thing before you act on it, and know who told you. A report is worth what its bearer is worth.

Back home, you want to know what the two men are like, and nobody is going to tell you. Someone could find out.

**Chose:** Ask Brother Robert, who confesses half the county

## August, 1400: Persuasion through others

> Your father decides. That is the law and the custom. But a wise woman knows that decisions are made by people, and people can be moved.

Your father leans toward Messire Hue de Cramailles: Cramailles holds 11 holdings to Avelin's 10, and asks no less in dowry. Your mother asks you, privately, which match you would choose if you could.

**Chose:** Tell her you will be content with your father's choice

## September, 1400: Persuasion through others

> Never ask for a thing yourself when someone he trusts can ask for it better. And never send someone who does not want it as much as you.

You have left the choice to your father, and he will take Cramailles. But the terms are still open: how much he pays, and what he asks for you in return. Someone can speak to him about that.

**Chose:** Let your mother speak to him

## October, 1400: Consequences arrive late

> What you do today, you will meet again in five years, wearing different clothes. Choose as if you will have to live beside it.

At the queen's court for the feast of Saint Denis, Ysabel de Fontenoy and Jacqueline d'Aubercourt quarrel over who should walk first behind the queen's sister-in-law. Words are said. Both name you as a witness. You saw it all.

**Chose:** Say you saw nothing

## December, 1400: The contract

> A marriage is a contract between two houses, and the contract is what protects you when the man does not. Read it. Have it read to you twice.

Your father has settled on Messire Hue de Cramailles. You left it to him. In the Duke of Berry's service. Pleasant, unambitious, a little slow. The dowry agreed is 2300 livres. The wedding is set for the spring, after your eighteenth birthday.

The lawyers have drawn the contract in the usual form. In return for the dowry you are to swear that you will claim nothing more of your father's lands, now or after his death. Daughters do; few think about it. There is also the matter of your dower, should you be widowed.

**Chose:** Sign, and ask for your dower to be assigned on named lands

### April 1401 · The wedding

In April 1401, at eighteen, you marry Messire Hue de Cramailles in the chapel at Brécourt, where your father and his father were married. Your father pays 2300 livres. Your husband holds 11 holdings and belongs to no party. You leave your mother's house with Perrette, two chests, and the book of hours your mother gave you, with wide margins, "for your own thoughts, which nobody else will want."

- Act Two. The young wife at court, 1401 to 1407. You have a husband, a household and a name to build. The princes are already quarrelling.

**Chose:** Begin

### April 1401 · What you want

### Spring 1402 · The Romance of the Rose (Christine)

Christine de Pizan is at court with a bundle of letters tied in red. For a year she has been arguing in writing with Jean de Montreuil, the provost of Lille, and the Col brothers, one of them a secretary to the King, about the Romance of the Rose: Jean de Meun's great poem, which every gentleman at the Court of Love can quote, and which says that every woman is for sale and every wife a trap.

"They write ballades swearing to defend ladies' honour," she says, "and then they defend that book. I want the queen to read both sides. I cannot walk into her chamber. You can."

### Winter, 1407 · In the margins of her book of hours

I have learned to count to ten before I speak, and some days to a hundred. Cramailles is what I was told he would be: no better, no worse, and away a good deal. Robert is living, and one is in the churchyard. Master Lorfèvre says the Parlement is slow but not deaf. It has heard us three times, and Gérard still sleeps in my father's bed. In November they killed the Duke of Orléans in the street, and the whole city held its breath, and then let it out again. I lied for Ysabel before the queen's women. God forgive me, I would do it again. Jacqueline has the queen's ear at night. I can tell when she has used it. I carried Christine's letters against the Romance of the Rose into the queen's chamber. The King's secretary has not smiled at me since. Good. I know what the king said to the queen that night. I have never told anyone, and she knows it.

- What is said of her: careful, very warm.

**Chose:** Turn the page

## Autumn 1422 · on the estates

Actions: accounts, repair

- You went through the rolls with Jehan Malet.
- The house is repaired and watched. Household +15.
- The council gives your husband a place in the king's household, from the Burgundians: 220 livres a half-year while you are at court to collect it.
- Christine de Pizan has answered your letter.
- Your husband's place in the king's household: unpaid this half-year, because you were away. Another half-year away and it will go to someone else.

### Autumn 1422 · The king is dead

On 21 October Charles VI dies at the Hôtel Saint-Pol, after forty-two years as king and thirty of them mad. Few of the great lords follow his body to Saint-Denis. A herald cries long life to Henry, King of France and England. South of the Loire, the Dauphin calls himself king.

## The end

Item, of Marguerite of the house of Brécourt, born in the year 1383, married at eighteen to Messire Hue de Cramailles. She wanted to see two of her children placed in good houses, and did not have it. Her lord came home from Agincourt, which few did. She bore 4 children, of whom 2 were living at the end. Robert was promised to the house of Montrevel. She made peace with her cousin over Brécourt by a marriage, so that her grandchildren would hold it. She hid her friend Ysabel de Fontenoy from the Armagnacs at the risk of her own house. She took in her enemy Jacqueline d'Aubercourt and her children when they had nowhere to go, and was repaid. She went with Queen Isabeau into exile at Tours, when few would. She kept her husband's oath to his brother-in-arms, Raoul de Fléchin, when it was dangerous to keep. Her lord trusted her judgment, and said so. Her house held more land at the end than at the beginning. She was a lady well spoken of, and was called very devout, bold and very warm. She outlived the mad king, which was more than many managed.

"I could not place them as I wanted. I could keep them alive. Most years that was enough."
