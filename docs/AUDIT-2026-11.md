# Audit: The Lady of Saint-Pol (2026-11-14)

What can be polished, expanded or made more entertaining. Three reviews:
1. Against the Game-Workshop playbook (failure catalogue F1-F38, release checklist).
2. Against the 10 sibling games in this workspace (what they have that this lacks).
3. As a player, from four full playthroughs (`docs/playthroughs/`), two new
   homebody runs, and two 100-run batches.

Nothing was changed by the audit. Claims marked **verified** were re-checked by hand.

## Verdict
The writing, the late payoffs and the indirect-power system are strong and worth
protecting. Three problems hold the game back:
- **It doesn't tell you what happened.** The result of a story choice is never shown.
- **Playing safe wins.** Staying on the estates for 22 years scores best and almost never dies.
- **The routine repeats.** The same report lines and cards come back word for word.

Release work (itch, a live Pages link, the icon) has not started.

## Tier 2: what makes it more entertaining (medium)
### 2.1 Playing safe should cost something you feel
- **Numbers:** homebody scores 83 and survives 96-98%, with a median purse of about 8,600-9,000 livres. Christine (court, reads the news) scores 74.5 and survives 84%. Courtier: 63-66, survives 61%.
- **Why:**
  - Nothing in the score rewards being at court (`derive.js:308-327`).
  - Wealth stops counting at about 4,000 livres.
  - Story beats still reach her at home, with text that assumes Paris: in one homebody run she "walked into the Châtelet yourself".
- **Fix, in this order:**
  - Some beats depend on where she is, with a home version that is smaller and later.
  - A court part of the legacy score (offices held, the queen's trust, children placed well).
  - Children's best matches need court favour (kill-gate K1).
  - Re-measure at 400 runs.

### 2.2 The half-year plan is solved
- On the estates, "Go through the accounts" is free and adds 15% to rents. One homebody run took it 43 of 44 turns.
- At court the plan is always attend, petition, and a gift or an office.
- Spreading a rumour (40 livres to cut the rival's standing, which feeds one event's weight), opening letters and replacing a servant are never worth it.
- **Fix:**
  - Accounts gets weaker when repeated, because the steward is already watched.
  - Rumours and opened letters get concrete story results: a rival loses a match, an office, or a lawsuit.

### 2.3 Repetition
- **Report lines** (per run):

  | Line | Times per run |
  |---|---|
  | "You waited on the queen" | 20-39 |
  | "You went through the rolls" | 11-43 |
  | Letter to Christine and her answer | 14 each |
  | "Unpaid... because you were away" | 13 |

  **Fix:** fold routine results into one summary sentence and report only changes and surprises. Give each action 3-4 wordings.
- **Cards:**
  - The birth card is word for word, up to four times a run, with the same wet-nurse question each time.
  - "A fever" appears up to four times, and all three of its choices only cost health.
  - "Christine asks a favour" appears three times about the same new book.
  - "A letter from Christine" appears four times, with one choice ("Keep the letter").

  **Fix:** once-only or variant text. Make the fever choices actually differ (a physician costs money, a saint's relic costs a debt, the estates air costs time away).

### 2.4 A quarter of the taps are "Continue"
26-28 of the 102-114 decisions in a run are history cards with no choice.
**Fix:** fold minor turning points into the half-year report. Give the big ones a reaction choice: the murder of Orléans, the Cabochiens, Agincourt, Troyes.

### 2.5 Act 4 goes flat
- From 1419 to 1422 there is mostly Continue and routine.
- Troyes "passes you by".
- A careful player dies only of childbed or the 1418 sickness, never by her own decisions.

**Fix:** one Act 4 test she cannot avoid by staying home. Options:
- Swearing to the Treaty of Troyes or refusing.
- English soldiers billeted on the estates.
- A claim on her dower.

Plus something worth spending a fortune on (a chapel, a town house, a daughter's great match).

### 2.6 News is still worth little
The "deaf" bot, which ignores all news, scores the same or better (80 vs 74.5).
**Fix:** a believed warning does more than send her home:
- buy a safe-conduct;
- move her money to a Lombard;
- get a child out of a household that is about to fall.

### 2.7 Endings converge
All six measured epitaphs:
- made peace over Brécourt by a marriage;
- took in Jacqueline and were repaid;
- kept the oath to Raoul.

Three of the six end on the same last line. Part of this is the bots choosing alike. The rest is that every run gets the same six storylines in the same order.

**Fix:**
- Beats that exist on only one branch.
- Epitaph sentences for rarer outcomes.
- Grade thresholds set from the measured spread: 60 of 100 bot games, random included, earned the top grade, "A house that rose".

Left out on purpose:
- **Action minigames such as jousting.** Her power is indirect; she does not hold the lance.
- **Achievement lists.** They clash with the chronicle's voice; one historical honour would fit.
- **Generated portraits.** Many of the cast are real people, and portraits don't suit the manuscript look.
- **A large world map.** The game is centred on Paris.

## What to protect
1. **The voice:** concrete, dry, domestic. "He checks the candle ends." "Your father had no debts... He had me." Only one anachronism was found, "a very long investment", in the 1415 margin entry.
2. **Early choices pay off late:** the 1400 renunciation clause leads to the 1403 seizure, then the lawsuit, the 1418 appeal and the 1420 settlement. The recall lines make each callback land.
3. **The book-of-hours margin pages**, written from her own choices.
4. **History with teeth:** pensions stop, fines, confiscations, the Châtelet.
5. **Power through servants:** gifts delivered late, stewards skimming, letters opened at a gate.
6. **The engineering:**
   - a pure, seeded engine;
   - versioned saves v1-v5 with fixtures;
   - purse equal to the ledger;
   - the phone smoke test;
   - the style lint.
